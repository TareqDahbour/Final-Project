Business Logic Flaws (CWE-841)

Nothing is “broken” in the code, yet the application can be made to do things its designers never intended.

1. What is it?

A business logic flaw is a mistake in the rules of an application rather than in how it handles bytes. CWE-841 (Improper Enforcement of Behavioral Workflow) describes the common case: the application expects users to follow a sequence of steps, or to supply sensible values, but never actually checks.

There is no payload and no scanner signature. The attacker simply uses the feature in an unexpected way: a negative quantity, a discount code used ten times, a payment step that can be skipped.

Real-world impact. Free or heavily discounted goods, credit added to wallets, bypassed payments and limits, and loyalty-point farming. These flaws are invisible to automated tools, so they are often found by attackers first.

2. How it works

  1. Unchecked quantity. The cart accepts -5 of a product. Its price is subtracted from the total, so a negative line item cancels out the price of an expensive one.
  2. Stackable coupon. A 20% coupon is applied every time it is submitted. Ten submissions leave about 11% of the price.
  3. Broken workflow. The total is locked when you click “Review order”, but payment uses that stale number even if the cart changes afterwards. Add a cheap item, review, add the expensive item, then pay the cheap price.

3. How to fix it

Why it works.

  • Validate every input on the trusted side. Quantities must be whole numbers inside an allowed range. The client is never the authority.
  • Recalculate from trusted data. Prices come from your own price list, and the total is recomputed at payment time rather than trusted from an earlier step.
  • Make one-time things one-time. Track coupon use as state and refuse repeats.
  • Enforce the workflow. Each step checks that the previous one happened, and any change to the cart invalidates the review so it cannot go stale.
  • Write down the business rules as invariants (“a total is never negative”) and test that attackers cannot break them.
