LAB 2 · HANDS-ON

Business Logic: Mini Shop

Your goal You have $100 in your wallet. Complete a checkout for far less than the goods are worth (for example the $500 Laptop Pro), or end up with a negative total. The whole shop runs in your browser.

Hints

Hint 1: What does the shop trust?

Look at the order log and the Active code. The shop never questions the quantity box, the coupon button or the review step. Try values a real shopper would never enter.

Hint 2: Three different weaknesses

There are at least three ways in: a quantity that is not a sensible positive number, a coupon that is not single-use, and a workflow where the price is locked in one step but charged in another.

Hint 3: The workflow trick

Add something cheap, click Review order, then change the cart before you pay. Does the price follow your changes?

Show solution

With the switch OFF, any one of these works:

  1. Negative quantity. Add Laptop Pro × 1, then add Headphones with quantity -5. The total is $500 − $400 = $100, exactly your wallet. Place the order. (A fractional quantity such as 0.01 also works.)
  2. Coupon stacking. Add Laptop Pro × 1 and submit SAVE20 ten times. The total drops to about $53.69. Place the order.
  3. Stale review. Add Sticker Pack × 1, click Review order (total locked at $5.00), then add Laptop Pro × 1 and click Place order. You pay $5.00.

Now turn the switch ON and repeat any of them: each is rejected and the order log explains which rule stopped it. You can even build the cheating cart first, then flip the switch and press Place order.

Fix the vulnerability

OFF runs the vulnerable code. ON runs the patched code. Exploit, flip, then retry.
